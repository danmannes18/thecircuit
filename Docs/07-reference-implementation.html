Logline

Inside the billion-dollar world of international show jumping — where Olympic athletes, dynastic heirs, and the horses that bind them chase the sport's ultimate prize across a year on the road to the 2028 Los Angeles Games.

The World

There is a world most people have never seen.

It moves in a circle. Wellington in winter. Aachen in summer. The Hamptons in late August. Geneva by Christmas. The same eighty or so faces — the riders, the owners, the grooms, the families — show up at each stop, set up their barns, compete for millions, and leave for the next city. They live their entire lives inside this orbit. They call it the circuit.

It is one of the wealthiest professional sports on earth, and one of the least televised. A single horse can sell for fifteen million dollars. A season campaigning at the top level costs more than a Premier League salary. The grooms who care for these animals know them better than any human in their lives. The riders are athletes, but they are also showmen, businesspeople, and in many cases, the children of the most powerful families in the world. Bruce Springsteen's daughter is on the U.S. Olympic team. Bill Gates' daughter married an Egyptian Olympian. Steve Jobs' daughter is a working international rider. They compete against the working professionals who built their careers from nothing — McLain Ward, Kent Farrington, Laura Kraut — riders who have spent thirty years getting to where the heirs arrived as teenagers.

In 2028, the Olympics come home. For the first time since 1984, the equestrian Games will be held on American soil, at Galway Downs in Southern California. American show jumping has not produced an individual Olympic gold medalist since Joe Fargis won in Los Angeles forty-four years earlier. Every rider on the circuit — American, European, Middle Eastern, South American — is now building toward one stadium and a single weekend in July 2028.

The Circuit is a two-season premium documentary series that follows that journey. Season one, filmed across 2027, builds the world: the year-long tour, the rivalries, the bloodlines, the partnerships between human and horse that define the sport. Season two, filmed across 2028, follows the same characters as their orbit narrows toward Los Angeles — through team selection, qualification trials, late-breaking injuries, and finally the Olympic stadium itself.

The horse is the only Olympic athlete that doesn't know it's competing. That, more than anything, is what makes this sport cinema.

The Case

Why this series. Why now.

01

Documentary sports has become prestige television.

Drive to Survive. Full Swing. Break Point. Quarterback. Beckham. Race for the Crown. Tour de France: Unchained. The genre has matured into the most reliable subscriber-driver in streaming. Show jumping — the most photogenic, most globally distributed of all horse sports — has never been given the premium-doc treatment.

02

The 2028 Los Angeles Olympics is a fixed, marketable finale.

The first home Olympics for American equestrians since 1984. A two-season series that climaxes there is a buyer's dream: a clean structure, a fixed delivery date, and a guaranteed cultural moment to release into.

03

The cast is already famous.

Springsteen, Jobs, Gates, Bloomberg, Selleck, Cuoco. The children of the people who built modern America are already in this world. So are the working-class kids from Texas and Chicago who out-rode them. The collision is the show.

04

No one else has the access.

This is a world that runs on relationships, generational trust, and quiet introductions. Whoever gets in first owns this story.

The Team

The Circuit is in development with creative leadership being assembled with care. The project is being structured for partnership with a premium documentary production company in the tradition of Box to Box Films, Religion of Sports, Words + Pictures, and Imagine Documentaries.
