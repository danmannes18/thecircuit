# PROJECT: UNTITLED EQUESTRIAN SERIES
## Premium Cinematic Documentary — Show Bible & Casting Prompt v1.1

---

> **What this is:** An **unscripted cinematic documentary series**. Real riders. Real barns. Real horses. Real road to LA28.
>
> **What this is not:** Not scripted. Not docudrama. Not reenactment. Not a season-recap show. Not a beginner's guide to the sport.

---

## 1. THE PITCH

A premium, cinematic, character-driven documentary series about the global high-performance equestrian world — show jumping, dressage, and eventing — built around a single organizing question: **Who will make it to Los Angeles 2028, and what does it cost them to get there?**

It is a show about money, lineage, sacrifice, fear, partnership, and the bond between a human and a 1,200-pound animal whose career often lasts longer than a marriage. It moves where the season moves — Wellington in winter, Aachen in summer, Spruce Meadows in the fall — and treats the world's great horse properties (Castle Hill, Heritage Farm, Helgstrand, Carl Hester's yard, Boyd Martin's Windurra) as estates filmed with the patience and reverence of *The Last Cowboy* or *Buck* — real places, real working families, captured cinematically.

The road to LA28 — Santa Anita Park, July 14–29, 2028 — is the spine. Six gold medals across three disciplines. Two hundred Olympic quota spots. Hundreds of horse-and-rider partnerships chasing them.

Where other equestrian projects in development chase the weekend-to-weekend competitive drama, this one moves at the speed of a season, a career, a horse's lifetime. An unscripted cinematic docu-series in the lineage of *The Last Dance*, *Free Solo*, and *Buck* — real people, real barns, real horses, real stakes — built for the audience that turned those titles into prestige category-definers.

## 1A. HOW THIS SHOW RELATES TO THE PREMIER JUMPING LEAGUE

In March 2027, the **Premier Jumping League (PJL)** launches — Frank McCourt's $300M, 16-team, 14-venue global jumping league, with broadcast produced by Box to Box Films (the Emmy-winning team behind *Drive to Survive*). It is the biggest commercial development in equestrian sport in a generation.

**This show is built to run alongside it, not against it.** The PJL covers the league. We cover the world the league lives in.

### Why the two projects don't compete

| | **PJL × Box to Box** | **This Show** |
|---|---|---|
| **Center of gravity** | The league. Teams. Standings. Season arc. | The people, horses, and houses behind the sport. |
| **Discipline** | Show jumping only. | All three Olympic disciplines: jumping, dressage, eventing. |
| **Time scale** | Weekend to weekend. Race-by-race drama. | Months, seasons, careers, horse lifetimes. |
| **Storytelling unit** | Events and rivalries inside one season. | Family dynasties, partnerships, the road to LA28. |
| **Tone reference** | *Drive to Survive* — fast, propulsive, conflict-forward. | *The Last Dance* / *Free Solo* / *Buck* — slow, painterly, intimate. |
| **Distribution model** | Free-to-view, mass reach, sport-fan acquisition. | Premium / prestige, subscription, character-doc audience. |
| **Olympic relationship** | Parallel commercial track. | LA28 is the spine. |

The PJL is built to recruit new fans into the sport. This show is built to give those new fans — and the audience that already exists — the deeper world the league points to. **Their show makes the league. This show makes the world the league lives in.**

### Why this is a strength in the pitch room

A buyer betting on equestrian content in the LA28 cycle wants two things: the new mass-market entry point (PJL) and the prestige character-driven series that follows the fan deeper. They are companion products in a flywheel, not substitutes. The PJL gives the sport its *F1 moment*. This show gives it its *Last Dance moment*.

There is recent, direct precedent for this exact pairing: Formula 1 itself runs the race weekends *and* hosts *Drive to Survive* as the character-driven prestige layer. The NBA runs the league *and* allowed *The Last Dance*. The PGA Tour runs the events *and* greenlit *Full Swing*. Equestrian is about to have its league moment. The character-doc lane is structurally open, and best filled by a production that is explicitly not trying to be the league's show.

### What we share with the PJL (and lean into)

1. **Athlete sustainability.** McCourt has stated the PJL's core mission is creating a viable path for top riders to earn a living competing. This show humanizes that economics — the rider sleeping in the truck at 23, the rider with the $5M owner at 33, the rider whose career ended at 28 because a horse died. The PJL describes the problem in press releases; we film it.
2. **Horse welfare.** The PJL's Chief Equestrian Advisor, Lisa Lazarus, has stated publicly that "there is no greatness in horse sport without the welfare and safety of the horse." The welfare conversation is one of the sport's most charged moments — Dujardin's 2024 suspension, the Helgstrand controversies, the eventing format debate at LA28. We treat it as a real storyline, not a press release.
3. **Global growth.** The PJL spans Europe, North America, and the Middle East. So do our characters — Mexico, Israel, Dominican Republic, Germany, Denmark, the UK, the US.
4. **Riders in common.** Lillie Keenan is already publicly named as a PJL supporter. So are many of our other show-jumping leads. Both productions can shoot the same riders without overlap because we're shooting different things — they're shooting their league rounds; we're shooting their lives, their barns, their families, and the other two disciplines they may also touch.

### What we deliberately don't do

- **We don't cover PJL events as PJL events.** Their broadcast and Box to Box have those rights and that lane. When our riders compete at a PJL stop, we're at the barn, the airport, the trailer, the home in Wellington afterward — not in the booth.
- **We don't break PJL news or do PJL standings recaps.** No "this week's results" cuts.
- **We don't recreate their format.** No team-by-team subplots, no "rivalry of the week" structure.
- **We don't make villains out of the league or its leadership.** Buyers will want the optionality of partnership later; we keep that door open.

### The pitch sentence

*"The PJL × Box to Box is the league's show. This is the sport's show. Their characters are teams; ours are dynasties, marriages, fathers and daughters, horses, and the LA28 Olympic dream the league itself is built around."*

## 2. WORLD-BUILDING PILLARS

The series is built on five pillars. Every episode should touch at least three.

### Pillar 1 — The Sport
Jumping, dressage, eventing. The Olympic Games are the apex but the calendar runs year-round through Wellington (Jan–Apr), the European summer (May–Sep), Spruce Meadows, the indoor fall (Aachen, Stuttgart, Geneva), and the Major League / Longines Global Champions Tour circuits.

### Pillar 2 — The Horse
Every great rider is half of a partnership. Sapphire (McLain Ward's two-time Olympic gold mare) had her own career arc, retirement, and biography. Valegro (Charlotte Dujardin's dressage horse) had a state funeral when he retired. Horses have personalities — anxious, brave, mareish, lazy in the warm-up but electric in the ring — and the show treats them as co-leads, not props.

### Pillar 3 — The Family
Equestrian sport is dynastic in a way almost no other sport is. The Wards, the Madden family, the Beerbaums, the Whitakers, the Hank Guerreiros, the Springsteens, the Jobses, the Propps, the Cooks — fathers, mothers, daughters, sisters, husbands, wives. Many marriages and rivalries are internal to the sport. Many barns are family businesses passed down two and three generations.

### Pillar 4 — The Money
A Grand Prix horse costs $500K to $5M+. A top dressage horse can clear $10M. Owners, sponsors, and syndicates underwrite riders the way Formula 1 teams underwrite drivers — except the "car" is alive, mortal, and forms emotional attachments to one person. The show should not shy from this: the Wellington compound, the private jets that fly horses between continents, the watch deals (Richard Mille, Rolex, Longines), the Hermès saddles, the Hampton Classic VIP tents.

### Pillar 5 — The Cost
Falls. Career-ending injuries to horses and riders. Liz Halliday's 2024 accident that ended her Olympic campaign and transferred her horses to Boyd Martin. Kevin Babington's 2019 fall that left him paralyzed. The mental weight of fear. The cost of missing your kids' childhoods to be in Wellington for four months a year. The loss of a horse you've ridden for a decade.

## 3. THE CHARACTER ATLAS

Cast in three tiers. The pilot should feature 4–6 leads drawn from at least two tiers and two disciplines.

### TIER 1 — THE LEGENDS
*The ones whose stories anchor the season. They've already won; the question is what they're chasing now.*

**McLain Ward (USA, Show Jumping)** — Six-time Olympian, two team golds, three silvers, former world #1. Operates Castle Hill Farm in Brewster, NY and Wellington, FL with wife Lauren (former pro) and their two daughters. Trained by his late father Barney, who served prison time in the 1990s for an insurance-fraud horse-killing scheme — a backstory that sits inside every shot of Castle Hill. Lillie Keenan trains out of his barn. The McKeever family (groom Lee, his wife Erica, their kids) has been at Castle Hill 30+ years. *Untouchable cinematic potential. The "captain" of American show jumping.*

**Kent Farrington (USA, Show Jumping)** — Won the 2026 FEI World Cup Final in Fort Worth. Self-made, no horse-family pedigree, built TeamKPF from the ground up. The American counterweight to Ward — driven, private, ruthless on course. The Bjorn Borg to Ward's Federer.

**Laura Kraut (USA, Show Jumping)** — 60+ years old, won team silver in Paris at 58, the oldest US woman to medal at the Olympics since 1904. Lives in Royal Palm Beach. Long-time mentor to Jessica Springsteen. The wisdom-and-grit character.

**Isabell Werth (Germany, Dressage)** — The greatest dressage rider alive. More Olympic medals than any equestrian in history. Currently ranked World #3 on her mare Wendy de Fontaine. Operates out of Rheinberg, Germany. The Serena Williams of her discipline.

**Cathrine Laudrup-Dufour (Denmark, Dressage)** — World #1 as of April 2026 on her 17-year-old Hanoverian mare Mount St John Freestyle. Made the largest leap to #1 in ranking history (from 25th). Calls Freestyle her "soul partner." Open about the spiritual dimension of the dance.

**Charlotte "Lottie" Fry (UK, Dressage)** — Former World #1, rides Glamourdale (a stallion famous in his own right for his electric movement). Charlotte Dujardin protege. The British dressage scene runs through her now that Dujardin has stepped back.

**Boyd Martin (USA, Eventing)** — World #1 eventer as of mid-2025 — the first American at #1 in 21 years. Australian-born, naturalized American. Wife Silva is a top dressage rider; their son rides. Operates Windurra USA in Pennsylvania. Inherited multiple horses including Miks Master C from Liz Halliday after her career-ending 2024 accident — a storyline with deep emotional charge.

**Michael Jung (Germany, Eventing)** — Triple Olympic gold medalist. His horse fischerChipmunk FRH is currently the #1-ranked eventing horse in the world. Quietly the most decorated eventing rider of his generation.

**Tom McEwen (UK, Eventing)** — Held world #1 for over a year through 2024–2025. Olympic individual silver in Tokyo on Toledo de Kerser. His horse JL Dublin is a fan favorite.

### TIER 2 — THE CONTENDERS (the LA28 Generation)
*The riders whose Olympic dream is genuinely open right now. Each one is a season-long arc.*

**Lillie Keenan (USA, Show Jumping)** — NYC-raised, the most decorated junior rider of her era (Maclay, USEF Medal, Hunter Derby Finals at 14). Trains at Castle Hill under Ward. Anchored the 2025 US team at Aachen with the only double-clear. Her main horses: Argan de Beliard (steady veteran) and Kick On (younger, electric, still learning). Tier-2 lead with Tier-1 résumé.

**Jessica Springsteen (USA, Show Jumping)** — Olympic silver, Tokyo 2020. Duke grad. Daughter of Bruce and Patti Scialfa. Trains with Edwina Tops-Alexander. Grew up on Stone Hill Farm, Colts Neck NJ. The celebrity-family hook every premium series wants. Her main horse: Don Juan van de Donkhoeve (her Tokyo partner).

**Eve Jobs (USA, Show Jumping)** — Steve and Laurene Powell Jobs' youngest daughter. Stanford history grad. Ranked World #5 under-25 in 2019. Recently married British Olympian Harry Charles. Now models for Glossier alongside competing. The "second-gen wealth meets Olympic ambition" character. Her career-defining horse: Venue d'Fees des Hazalles, her Pan Am bronze partner.

**Karl Cook (USA, Show Jumping)** — Son of Intuit founder Scott Cook. Married to actress Kaley Cuoco. Olympic team silver Paris 2024. Operates Pomponio Ranch in California. Charismatic, opinionated, a natural on-camera presence.

**Brian Moggré (USA, Show Jumping)** — Texan, young, electric. ASPCA Maclay reserve champion. Multiple FEI grand prix wins on MTM Vivre Le Reve. The next-gen American answer to Ward.

**Carlos Hank Guerreiro (Mexico, Show Jumping)** — Paris 2024 Olympian. Grandson of one of Mexico's most powerful business dynasties (his grandfather Carlos Hank Rhon, his father Carlos Hank González). Based in Belgium, married to Belgian rider Zoe Conter. Mexican team competes with a famously loud, passionate fan base — "two of my best friends were on the team with me in Paris; we've competed together since we were children." The H5 family operation includes his father and sister also competing.

**Richard Vogel (Germany, Show Jumping)** — World top 10. Lost his mother at 12; horses became his connection to her. Partners with David Will in a stable in Pfungstadt. Reigning European Champion. His horse United Touch S is one of the most electric jumpers in Europe right now.

**Daniel Bluman (Israel, Show Jumping)** — Colombian-born, rides for Israel. Major League Show Jumping team champion two years running (the Trelawny Trailblazers). Geopolitically and personally interesting character.

**Yvonne Losos de Muñiz (Dominican Republic, Dressage)** — Three-time Olympian for the DR (Rio, Tokyo, Paris). Born in Nigeria. Trains in Wellington and Europe. The non-European, non-American dressage character — proves the sport's reach.

**Adrienne Lyle (USA, Dressage)** — Top-ranked American, qualified for the 2026 World Cup Final on Helix. Idaho roots. The quiet, technical Western answer to the European dressage establishment.

**Yasmin Ingham (UK, Eventing)** — 2022 World Champion at 25, on Banzai du Loir. The eventing answer to Lottie Fry.

**Laura Collett (UK, Eventing)** — Paris 2024 team gold, on London 52 — one of the world's top eventing horses. Survived a near-fatal accident at 21 that left her in a medically induced coma; lost sight in one eye. Her comeback arc is one of the great unwritten stories in the sport.

**Cosby Green (USA, Eventing)** — Currently top-10 in the world. Young American eventer rising fast. The next-gen American story alongside Boyd Martin's veteran arc.

### TIER 3 — THE NEXT WAVE (Juniors / Young Riders)
*The under-21 riders whose Olympic timeline is LA28 or Brisbane 2032. The "we discovered them first" tier.*

**Eloise Eisner (USA, Junior Hunter/Jumper)** — NYC-based junior rider, top of the 2025 WIHS junior jumper field. Forms a social-media duo with JJ Torano (also a top junior). The "in the climb right now" character.

**The Propp Sisters — Stella, Juliette, Clara (USA)** — Three siblings, all serious riders, run a horse-treat business together (AJCS Treats), share horses among themselves as they age out of divisions. Stella has aged into college; the younger two are still climbing. A naturally cinematic sibling dynamic with genuine sport ambition behind it.

**Skylar Wireman (USA, Show Jumping)** — California-based, won the 2023 Longines FEI World Cup Fort Worth as a teenager. Already representing US in international competition.

**JJ Torano (USA, Junior Hunter)** — Multiple WIHS junior hunter championships in 2025. The hunter-discipline counterweight to the jumper-heavy junior cast.

**Calvin Böckmann (Germany, Eventing)** — Young German eventer, top-20 in the world on The Phantom of the Opera. The European junior eventer answer.

## 4. THE BARNS & SETTINGS

The show needs settings, not just people. Each of these is a recurring location with its own ensemble.

**Castle Hill Farm, Brewster NY + Wellington FL** — McLain Ward's operation. Multi-generational, with the McKeever family embedded for 30+ years. Lillie Keenan trains here. Inheritance themes already loaded.

**Heritage Farm, Westchester NY** — Andre Dignelli's legendary equitation operation. Where Lillie Keenan grew up. Where many of America's top juniors are still made.

**Back Country Farm, Greenwich CT + Wellington FL** — Jill Shulman's mother-daughter operation. Daughter Sydney rides for Team Israel. Top-100 Longines ranking. Classic East Coast hunter-jumper pedigree.

**Ilan Ferder Stables, Wellington FL** — Ilan Ferder + Tal Milstein. The European import/sales business — million-dollar horses changing hands. The "money side" of the sport.

**Pomponio Ranch, California** — Karl Cook & Kaley Cuoco's operation. West Coast counterweight to the Florida-NY axis.

**Windurra USA, Pennsylvania** — Boyd Martin's eventing base. Cross-country focus, Australian-American family operation.

**Wellington International, Florida** — Not a barn — the global capital of show jumping for 12 weeks every winter. Saturday Night Lights. The Winter Equestrian Festival. Where the entire global sport relocates for a quarter of the year.

**Aachen, Germany (CHIO Aachen)** — The Wimbledon of show jumping. Held every July. The most prestigious show in the world.

**Spruce Meadows, Calgary** — The other great outdoor venue. The Masters tournament every September.

**Helgstrand Dressage, Denmark** — Andreas Helgstrand's operation; the most controversial and powerful sales barn in dressage. (Note: subject of welfare controversy in 2023 — handle thoughtfully.)

**Carl Hester's Yard, Gloucestershire** — Where Charlotte Dujardin and Lottie Fry came from. The factory floor of British dressage.

**Santa Anita Park, Arcadia CA** — The 1984 Olympic equestrian venue, returning as host for LA28. Built for racing, retrofitted for the Games. The destination the show is always pointed at.

**Galway Downs, Temecula CA** — Likely LA28 eventing cross-country venue (1 hour south of LA). 242 acres, working racehorse facility.

## 6. STORYLINES TO TRACK (Season 1)

The show should be structured as 6–8 episodes, each anchored by 2–3 storylines that intersect.

1. **The American Comeback in Eventing** — Boyd Martin at #1, riding the horses Liz Halliday can no longer ride, chasing the first US individual eventing gold in over two decades on home soil at LA28.

2. **The Castle Hill Dynasty** — McLain Ward's sixth Olympic cycle, his daughter Lilly competing in Pony Finals, Lillie Keenan training in his shadow, the question of who inherits American show jumping.

3. **The Mare and the Dane** — Cathrine Laudrup-Dufour and Mount St John Freestyle in what is almost certainly the horse's final Olympic cycle. The cost of letting go.

4. **The Junior Climb** — Eloise Eisner, the Propp sisters, and JJ Torano. The pony-to-Olympics ladder, compressed in time-lapse.

5. **The Global Game** — Carlos Hank Guerreiro flying between Mexico City, Wellington, and Belgium. Richard Vogel between Pfungstadt and Florida. Yvonne Losos de Muñiz competing for a country with no other dressage rider at her level.

6. **The League Arrives** — March 2027: the Premier Jumping League's inaugural season opens with 16 teams, 14 venues, and a $300M prize pot — the biggest commercial development in the sport's history. We follow our riders *as they live through it*: who gets drafted into a team, what the new economics mean for owner relationships, how a young rider's career trajectory changes when there's suddenly a sustainable middle class in the sport. The league is the financial weather system of our show's season; it is not our subject, but everyone in our cast feels it.

7. **The Welfare Question** — The growing pressure on equestrian sport from animal-welfare critics. Dujardin's 2024 suspension. The Helgstrand controversies. The IOC's pressure to revise eventing's format for LA28. The sport's reckoning with itself.

8. **The Family Business** — The Propps, the Hank Guerreiros (father + son + daughter all competing), Karl Cook & Kaley Cuoco at Pomponio, the Wards at Castle Hill. Every family is a different model of how this works.

## 7. TONE, AESTHETIC, REFERENCES

**Reference films & series (all unscripted documentary work):**
- *Free Solo* — intimate, fear-based filmmaking around physical risk
- *The Last Dance* — legacy and the weight of being the best, told through real archival access
- *Buck* — the bond between a horseman and the animal, working life filmed reverently
- *The Last Cowboy* — the texture of working horse country, real families on real land
- *Full Swing* — the access-driven character doc inside a sport's pro circuit
- *Drive to Survive* — useful counter-reference: faster, conflict-forward, race-by-race; this show is the opposite tonal end
- *Won't You Be My Neighbor?* — emotional gentleness around the human-animal bond
- *Cunningham* — shooting the dressage sequences as dance, not as sport
- *Senna* — long-form portrait built from real footage of a real athlete and the people around him

**Visual rules:**
- Horses get the same lighting and cinematography as humans. No B-roll cutaways.
- The barn is shot like a home. The tack room like a study.
- Slow motion is reserved for jumps, dressage extensions, and falls. Used sparingly.
- Interviews happen in horses' presence whenever possible. The horse is in the frame.
- Drone shots of properties (Castle Hill, Windurra, Pomponio) are establishing-shot grammar.
- Wellington should look glamorous; Aachen should look ancient; Spruce Meadows should look like the prairie.

**What the show is not:**
- Not scripted. Not docudrama. Not reenactment. Everything on screen happens.
- Not a season recap. Results matter only as far as they reveal character.
- Not a beginner's guide. Trust the audience.
- Not anti-luxury or anti-money. The wealth of this world is part of its texture, not its scandal.
- Not pro-sport propaganda. The welfare questions are part of the story, not omitted from it.

## 8. THE LA28 SPINE

Every episode references the countdown. The Games run July 14–29, 2028 at Santa Anita Park (Arcadia, CA), with eventing cross-country likely at Galway Downs (Temecula). Six gold medals. 200 quota places (75 jumping, 60 dressage, 65 eventing). 50+ nations competed in equestrian at Tokyo and Paris — LA28 will likely match or exceed.

**Qualification windows:**
- Eventing MERs must be obtained at CCI4*-L / CCI5*-L events through June 2028
- Jumping team qualification flows through the Longines League of Nations and CSIO5* events
- Dressage qualification flows through CDI5* events and the World Cup Final

Major waypoints the show should plan around:
- **2027 European season** (Aachen, Rotterdam, Falsterbo) — last full continental season before Olympic year
- **March–April 2028 Wellington** — final US qualification window
- **June 2028 FEI Nations Cup Aachen** — team selection closes
- **July 14, 2028 — Day One, Santa Anita**

The series finale should be the Closing Ceremony.
