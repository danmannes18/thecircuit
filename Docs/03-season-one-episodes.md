# THE CIRCUIT — SEASON ONE — 2027

### "The Circuit"

*Eight episodes. One year on the road. The world introduced.*

---

The structural premise of season one is the calendar itself. The sport's professional year has a natural shape — Wellington in winter, Europe in spring, the summer Majors, the Hamptons finale, the indoor circuit through autumn, Geneva at Christmas. Season one follows that calendar in order. Each episode is anchored to a real event the cast is traveling to anyway, which means production never has to manufacture stakes — the stakes are already on the calendar.

Each episode opens up two to three characters from the bible in depth. By the season finale, the audience has met everyone, learned the world, and seen the first quiet signals of Los Angeles 2028 on the horizon.

---

## EPISODE 1 — "WELLINGTON"

**Anchored to:** The opening weeks of the Winter Equestrian Festival, Wellington International, Florida. January 2027.

**Centers on:** Kent Farrington. Lillie Keenan. McLain Ward. Jill and Sydney Shulman. The Wellington world itself.

The series opens not on a competition, but on a darkened cargo plane somewhere over the Atlantic, full of horses flying from Europe to South Florida for the winter season. We meet a groom traveling in the hold. We follow the trucks from the airport. We arrive in Wellington at sunrise — the moment a town of 65,000 people becomes the global capital of show jumping for thirteen weeks.

The episode is structured as a guided tour of an alien world. Kent Farrington, the reigning world number one, gives us the professional's view: this is his office, his workplace, his year-round home. McLain Ward, returning from his injury, gives us the elder statesman's view: this is the place where American show jumping was built. Lillie Keenan gives us the rising generation's view: this is where I prove I belong on the team next year. Jill and Sydney Shulman, arriving at Back Country Farm's Wellington base, give us the working trainer's view: this is the engine that produces the next generation.

Underneath all of it, the financial reality is shown without being narrated. A $3 million horse walks past the camera. A Hermès tent sets up in the boutique village. A barn rents for half a million dollars for the season. Saturday Night Lights opens under the floodlights.

**Function:** Establish the world. Hand the audience the map.

---

## EPISODE 2 — "BLOODLINES"

**Anchored to:** Mid-WEF, Wellington. February 2027. The $300,000 Longines FEI Jumping World Cup Qualifier at Ocala.

**Centers on:** Jessica Springsteen. Eve Jobs. Jennifer Gates Nassar and Nayel Nassar. Georgina Bloomberg.

The heirs' episode. Each of these four women arrived in this sport differently — Bloomberg almost born into it, Springsteen choosing it despite her father's world, Jobs growing into it through Stanford, Gates marrying into it. The episode does not interview their famous parents. It interviews them.

The narrative spine is the World Cup Qualifier weekend at Ocala — a real competition with real selection-points consequences. We follow each rider through the week: schooling at home, transporting horses two hours north to the World Equestrian Center, the early rounds, the Sunday Grand Prix. We meet Nayel Nassar in his Egyptian team kit and learn that the Gates-Nassar marriage will send two flags to Los Angeles next year. We watch Eve Jobs warm up at six in the morning with her trainer. We sit with Bloomberg as she talks about her son.

The episode resists the easy story. These women are not punchlines about nepo babies. They are working riders. The episode lets them be that.

**Function:** Reframe the heirs from tabloid figures into athletes.

---

## EPISODE 3 — "THE MATCH"

**Anchored to:** Late WEF and the Wellington Nations Cup. February–March 2027.

**Centers on:** Ilan Ferder. A young rider being matched to a horse. The horse trade itself.

A horse arrives from Belgium. A teenage rider arrives from Texas. A family arrives from California with a wire transfer ready. Ilan Ferder is in the middle of all three.

This is the episode that explains how the sport actually works financially — not through graphics, but through the quiet, multilingual conversations that happen in barn aisles. We follow one specific deal across the episode: a Grand Prix prospect Ferder has spent two years scouting, now being shown to a potential owner. We see the trial rides. We hear the negotiation. We watch the wire clear. We meet the horse on the morning it is loaded onto a van bound for Wellington with a new name on its stall plate.

In parallel, the Wellington Nations Cup unfolds — the U.S. team competing on home ground. Ridland makes his first appearance, watching from the rail. The pieces of the larger Olympic puzzle begin to move.

**Function:** Show the audience where the money actually lives. Introduce Ridland.

---

## EPISODE 4 — "THE CROSSING"

**Anchored to:** The end of the Wellington season and the migration to Europe. April–May 2027.

**Centers on:** The grooms. The horses. The logistics that no one ever films.

The fourth episode is the show's first formal break from convention. After three episodes anchored to competition, episode four follows the *travel* — the moment the entire industry packs up Wellington and ships itself, by air, across the Atlantic.

We follow real grooms across the journey. The packing. The vet inspections. The drive to Miami International. The flight. The arrival in Liège, Belgium, the unofficial Ellis Island of the show jumping horse. The week of recovery in European barns before the season begins.

The episode is quieter than the others. It is the breath the show takes before the European leg begins. It is also a statement of values: the horses and the people who care for them are the soul of *The Circuit*, and a whole episode is built around them before any of the famous names get another scene.

**Function:** Anchor the show's moral center. Earn the prestige label.

---

## EPISODE 5 — "AACHEN"

**Anchored to:** CHIO Aachen, Germany. Late June / early July 2027. The Rolex Grand Prix.

**Centers on:** Jan Tops. The European wing of the sport. McLain Ward and Laura Kraut on the world's most iconic grass arena.

Aachen is to show jumping what Wimbledon is to tennis. The grass is older than most of the riders. The Rolex Grand Prix purse is over two million euros. The stadium holds 40,000 people. Win here once and your career changes forever.

The episode opens with Jan Tops walking the grounds before competition begins — the Dutch impresario in his natural habitat, in his home language, surrounded by the European old guard. Through him, we meet the European wing of the sport: the German engineering, the Dutch trading houses, the Belgian breeding farms, the Irish jumping families that have produced champions for four generations.

Then the Americans arrive, and the episode pivots. McLain and Laura ride at Aachen every year. They know the grass. They know the warm-up ring. They are old friends with riders the audience has never heard of. The episode lets the American audience see European show jumping the way Americans see it — as both familiar and slightly alien, as the holy ground their heroes have been crossing the ocean for thirty years to compete on.

**Function:** Open the show out beyond America. Introduce the global scale of the sport.

---

## EPISODE 6 — "HAMPTONS"

**Anchored to:** The Hampton Classic Horse Show, Bridgehampton, New York. Labor Day week, late August 2027.

**Centers on:** Georgina Bloomberg. The social architecture of the sport. The intersection of horse, fashion, and East Coast society.

The Hampton Classic is the social peak of the equestrian summer. Fifty thousand attendees. A four-hundred-thousand-dollar Longines Grand Prix. Hermès tents. Champagne service. Real-estate brokers spending six figures on hospitality just to be seen.

The episode treats the social spectacle honestly — we are not pretending it isn't there — but it uses the spectacle to reveal something the audience has not yet seen. The Classic is where the sport's outer ring (the spectators, the sponsors, the celebrity ringside guests) collides with its inner ring (the riders, owners, grooms). The collision is the episode.

Bloomberg is the spine. She rides at the Classic every year, lives ten miles away, and knows everyone in both rings. She becomes our guide — through the VIP tent, the boutique village, the warm-up arena, and finally the Sunday Grand Prix itself. The episode closes with the trophy presentation as the sun sets over Bridgehampton, and the audience understands for the first time that this world is not separate from American power. It is American power.

**Function:** Establish that this sport exists at the center of American cultural and financial life, not at its margins.

---

## EPISODE 7 — "ROCKIES"

**Anchored to:** The Spruce Meadows 'Masters' Tournament, Calgary, Canada. September 2027. The CPKC International Grand Prix presented by Rolex.

**Centers on:** Brian Moggre. Mimi Gochman. The rising generation under pressure.

Spruce Meadows is the third Rolex Major of the year — North America's premier outdoor venue, set against the Canadian Rockies. The episode is the rising generation's episode. Moggre and Gochman are both campaigning for serious results that will push them toward Olympic selection contention. Both are young enough that their nerves are still visible. Neither has won a Major yet.

The episode is built around the long weekend at Spruce — the qualifying classes, the Nations Cup, the closing Sunday Grand Prix. We watch Moggre school in the morning fog with his trainer. We watch Gochman call her mother before a class. We watch them both ride, and one of them wins something significant.

In parallel, the older generation — McLain, Kent, Laura — is also competing at Spruce, and the episode quietly tracks the generational handoff happening in real time. The audience sees that the road to LA is already being built.

**Function:** Pivot the show toward season two. Make the audience care about the future, not just the present.

---

## EPISODE 8 — "GENEVA"

**Anchored to:** CHI Geneva, Switzerland. December 2027. The Rolex Grand Prix de Genève and the season's close.

**Centers on:** The full ensemble. The first explicit conversations about Los Angeles.

The season finale. Geneva in December — snow outside, the largest indoor arena in the world inside, the final Rolex Major of the calendar year, and the unofficial end of the show jumping season. Every major rider from every continent is in one building for one weekend.

The episode is structured as a series of conversations. McLain on whether he will pursue a seventh Olympics. Laura on whether her body and her best horse can hold together for another year. Kent on the qualification math. Jessica on what selection would mean to her and to her father. Eve on whether she will pursue a U.S. team spot or focus on the American Gold Cup circuit. Nayel and Jennifer on the prospect of competing against each other in Los Angeles.

Robert Ridland, finally given an extended sit-down interview, talks about the shape of the team he is starting to imagine. He names no names.

The final sequence is the Sunday Grand Prix. The trophy is presented. The lights go down on Geneva. The graphic comes up: **NEXT SEASON. LOS ANGELES. JULY 2028.**

Cut to black.

**Function:** Close the world. Open the road.

---

## SEASON ONE ARC SUMMARY

The audience that watches all eight episodes of season one will have, by the end:

1. Met fifteen anchor characters across four archetypes
2. Visited eight of the most important venues in the sport, across three continents
3. Learned the structure of the professional calendar
4. Understood how the financial machinery works without being lectured about it
5. Met the horses by name and recognized them across episodes
6. Watched the first Olympic-selection tensions emerge
7. Been positioned, narratively, for a second season they now want immediately

---

## A NOTE ON STRUCTURE

The episodes are designed to be modular. A viewer who drops in at episode five (Aachen) and has not seen episode one (Wellington) can still follow the show — each episode is built to stand alone while serving the season's larger arc. This matches the model of *Drive to Survive* and *Full Swing*, both of which are designed for non-linear viewing on streaming platforms.

Episode lengths target 50 minutes each, with episode four ("The Crossing") potentially running shorter at 40 minutes as a quiet interlude. Episode eight ("Geneva") may run longer, up to 60 minutes, as the season finale.
