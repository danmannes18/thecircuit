# THE CIRCUIT — SEASON TWO — 2028

### "Road to Los Angeles"

*Eight episodes. The same circuit, now bending toward a single stadium.*

---

The premise of season two is that nothing about the calendar has changed — and everything about the meaning of the calendar has changed. The Wellington season the audience watched in season one as a tour of a world is now, in season two, the opening battlefield of an Olympic year. Every class is a selection class. Every horse purchase is a pre-Games gamble. Every conversation between a rider and a Chef d'Equipe carries weight that a casual class never did.

The structural ambition of season two is to use the audience's familiarity with the world as fuel. The viewer no longer needs to be taught how a Grand Prix works. They know who Lillie Keenan's mentor is. They know what a horse is worth. They know that Robert Ridland is the man who decides. That knowledge is now the show's accelerator: every scene plays at higher altitude because the audience is finally an insider.

The season is structured around three acts. The opening four episodes are selection. The fifth and sixth episodes are the final pre-Games stretch. The seventh episode is the lead-in to Los Angeles itself. The eighth episode is the Games.

---

## EPISODE 1 — "ELECTION YEAR"

**Anchored to:** The opening of the 2028 Winter Equestrian Festival, Wellington. January 2028. The Palm Beach County Sports Commission Grand Prix.

**Centers on:** Robert Ridland. Kent Farrington. McLain Ward. The selection process itself.

The season opens with Ridland. We see him at his desk before he sees a single horse. We watch him walk into the warm-up ring at WEF on opening day. We hear him for the first time outline, on camera, what he is looking for — and explicitly *not* looking for — in a team headed to Los Angeles.

The episode then introduces the central question of the season: who, among the riders the audience already knows, will actually make the three-person Olympic team? Kent Farrington is the presumptive number one. McLain Ward, recovered from his 2026 injury, is positioning for a seventh Olympics that no American rider has ever achieved. Laura Kraut is publicly noncommittal about whether she will pursue a fifth Games or step back. Karl Cook is defending his Paris silver. The math is unforgiving: three spots, four established legends, and a generation of rising riders pushing from below.

The episode is structured as a series of conversations between Ridland and each of the contenders, intercut with footage of them riding in the opening WEF weeks. By the end of episode one, the audience has its first emotional ranking of the contenders — and that ranking is going to be wrong.

**Function:** Establish the season's central question. Make Ridland a character.

---

## EPISODE 2 — "INHERITANCE"

**Anchored to:** Mid-WEF, Wellington. February 2028. The Hermès U25 Grand Prix Series and the Nations Cup Wellington.

**Centers on:** Lillie Keenan. Brian Moggre. Mimi Gochman. Eve Jobs. The generation pushing from below.

The selection conversation widens. The Olympic team will not be three legends. It will be one or two legends, plus a rising rider — Ridland has said as much in season one. Episode two is the audition.

Keenan is the front-runner. She has been mentored explicitly for this. Moggre is the dark horse — Texas-born, working-class, the rider Ridland has been watching since he was sixteen. Gochman is the long shot, but a long shot whose family has invested everything in giving her this moment. Jobs is the genuinely uncertain element: a working international rider who has not declared whether she will pursue the U.S. team selection at all.

The episode follows all four through the Hermès U25 weekend and into the Nations Cup, where Ridland fields the team that will telegraph his thinking. Whoever rides in the Nations Cup is being looked at. Whoever doesn't, isn't.

The closing scene of the episode is Eve Jobs walking out of a quiet conversation with her trainer. She has made a decision. The audience does not yet know what it is.

**Function:** Open the field. Force the audience to choose a favorite.

---

## EPISODE 3 — "TWO FLAGS"

**Anchored to:** Late WEF and the European spring opener — the Longines League of Nations in Ocala, Florida, and the run-up to The Dutch Masters. March 2028.

**Centers on:** Jennifer Gates Nassar and Nayel Nassar. Sydney Shulman. Jessica Springsteen.

This is the international selection episode — the riders whose Olympic paths run through teams other than the United States, and the American riders whose decisions are now affecting other flags.

Nayel Nassar is campaigning for Egypt's selection list. His Olympic team has half the depth of the U.S. side, but the pressure is no less real — Egypt has not had a male show jumper on an Olympic podium in modern history. Jennifer has not announced whether she will pursue Olympic selection for the U.S. The dual-flag household — wife potentially riding under one banner, husband definitely riding under another, two young children at home — is one of the season's quiet anchor stories.

Sydney Shulman, riding for Israel, is in the same boat: a working international rider on a smaller team, where every qualification point is more transparent and more consequential than on the U.S. side. We watch Sydney at WEF, then at Ocala, then leaving for Europe. Jill watches her go. The mother-daughter shot the audience has been waiting for since season one finally lands.

Jessica Springsteen, meanwhile, is publicly committed to pursuing the U.S. team. She is the most famous person in this episode and the one with the most to lose if Ridland passes her over.

**Function:** Expand the season's stakes beyond Team USA. Establish that LA28 is a *global* convergence, not just an American one.

---

## EPISODE 4 — "THE EUROPEAN SUMMER"

**Anchored to:** The Dutch Masters, Den Bosch. March 2028. CHIO Aachen, June 2028. The spring and summer European circuit.

**Centers on:** Jan Tops. The international field. The Americans on European grass.

The Americans cross the Atlantic. The Europeans defend their home turf. The episode is the season's widest in geographic scope.

The Dutch Masters opens the European Major calendar. By June, every American Olympic hopeful is in Aachen, riding the same grass arena where they have either won big or lost ugly in past seasons. Aachen 2028 will be Ridland's last major observation event before the team is finalized. The episode follows the U.S. riders through the Aachen weekend with the explicit framing: this is the last meaningful audition.

Jan Tops returns as our guide to the European field. Through him we meet the rivals the U.S. team will face in Los Angeles — Christian Kukuk and Richard Vogel of Germany, Scott Brash and Ben Maher of Great Britain, Martin Fuchs of Switzerland, Henrik von Eckermann of Sweden. The episode treats them with the same dignity as the Americans. The Olympics is a global event. The show is, finally, behaving like one.

The final sequence of the episode is the Aachen Rolex Grand Prix. The result, whatever it is in real life, plays as the de facto end of the audition process.

**Function:** Make the global field as compelling as the American one. Set up selection.

---

## EPISODE 5 — "THE LIST"

**Anchored to:** The U.S. Olympic Show Jumping Team announcement. Early summer 2028.

**Centers on:** Robert Ridland. The riders who make the team. The riders who don't.

This is the episode every viewer has been waiting for. Ridland names three riders and one alternate to represent the United States at the Los Angeles Olympic Games. The episode is structured around the announcement itself — the days leading up to it, the day of, and the immediate aftermath.

The cast splits cleanly into two storylines. The riders who made the team — and the production has prepared multiple narrative branches here, because the real selection is unpredictable — are now headed to Los Angeles. Their preparation begins. Their horses' final fitness work begins. Their families begin to plan travel.

The riders who did not make the team have a different episode. Some are graceful. Some are not. Some are quietly devastated. Some pivot immediately to individual qualification spots that may still be available through ranking. The episode does not soften any of this. Olympic selection is brutal. The show has spent two seasons earning the right to film it honestly.

The episode closes with the team named, the alternate named, and the camera on Ridland walking away from the press conference. He has just made the most consequential decision of his career.

**Function:** The season's structural turning point. Everything after this episode is execution. Everything before it was politics.

---

## EPISODE 6 — "THE HORSES"

**Anchored to:** July 2028. The final pre-Olympic training period.

**Centers on:** The horses. The grooms. The veterinarians. The quiet weeks before the Games.

A deliberate pause. After five episodes of human politics, episode six is about the four-legged athletes — the only Olympic competitors who do not know they are about to compete.

The episode follows the U.S. team horses — Ilex, Greya, Baloutinue, and whichever new partnerships have emerged during the season — through their final preparation. The vet checks. The fitness work. The shoeing. The travel arrangements that, for a horse, are more complex than for the human teams. The flight from Liège to Los Angeles. The quarantine at the venue.

Jill Shulman has a beautiful scene in this episode. She is not at the Olympics. She is at home in Wellington, preparing the next generation of junior riders for their summer indoors. Her perspective on what the Games mean — earned across thirty-five years of running a barn — gives the audience the wider American context. The Olympics is not just three riders. It is a hundred thousand kids in barns across the country watching their heroes pack for California.

The episode is, by design, the quietest of the season. It is also, by design, the most emotionally loaded. The audience has spent two seasons earning the right to feel something when a horse is loaded onto a plane bound for Los Angeles.

**Function:** Slow the season down. Restore the horses to the center of the show. Earn the finale emotionally.

---

## EPISODE 7 — "LOS ANGELES"

**Anchored to:** The week before the Olympic Games. Galway Downs, Temecula, California. July 2028.

**Centers on:** The full ensemble. The arrival. The course walk. The opening ceremony.

The Games begin. Every rider, every horse, every Chef d'Equipe, every owner the audience has met across two seasons converges on Galway Downs, the equestrian venue for LA28, located in the wine country north of San Diego.

The episode is structured as the days before competition. Arrival at the venue. The first horse inspection. The U.S. team's first walk of the course. The opening ceremony, where the equestrian team marches with the broader U.S. delegation. The first conversations with international rivals the audience now knows by name.

This is also the episode in which the show finally puts its full ensemble in one frame. McLain and Lillie. Kent and Brian. Laura and Sydney. Jessica and Eve. Jennifer and Nayel. Jill watching from the stands. Ridland in his folding chair at the rim of the warm-up ring, finally watching the team he chose ride against the world.

The episode closes on the night before competition. The barn at midnight. The horses standing quietly. A groom walks the aisle one last time. The next shot is the morning of competition day.

**Function:** Set the stage. Convert two seasons of buildup into a single weekend.

---

## EPISODE 8 — "GOLD"

**Anchored to:** The Olympic show jumping competition. Galway Downs, California. The first week of August 2028.

**Centers on:** Everything. Everyone.

The finale. The episode follows the full arc of the Olympic show jumping competition — the team qualifier, the team final, the individual qualifier, the individual final. Across four days, twelve nations, thirty horses, and one stadium.

The episode is structured around the real-world results, which the production cannot script and would not want to. Whatever happens — whether Team USA wins gold, whether an American wins the first individual show jumping Olympic gold for the country since Joe Fargis in 1984, whether a heart-shattering eight-fault round costs a team the podium — the episode plays it honestly. *The Circuit* has spent sixteen episodes earning the audience's trust that it will not soften the result. The finale honors that contract.

The closing sequence is the only scripted element of the episode — a montage across the full two seasons, cutting between Wellington in winter 2027 and Galway Downs in summer 2028, between Aachen grass and California sand, between every member of the cast at their moments of struggle and their moments of triumph. The final shot is a horse, alone, walking out of the arena in the late California light. The credits roll over the empty stadium.

If the show ends here, it ends complete.

**Function:** Honor the journey. Deliver the climax. Earn the goodbye.

---

## SEASON TWO ARC SUMMARY

The audience that watches the full sixteen episodes of *The Circuit* will have:

1. Followed fifteen anchor characters across two years, three continents, and one Olympic Games
2. Learned the structure of an entire elite sport from the inside out
3. Watched a real selection process play out, with real winners and real losers
4. Witnessed the most American Olympics in a generation through the lens of a sport almost no American knows
5. Met the horses by name and felt their results as deeply as the riders'
6. Been delivered the kind of season-finale catharsis that makes a show culturally permanent

---

## A NOTE ON POSSIBLE EXTENSION

The two-season structure is designed to be complete in itself. If the series is successful and a third season is commissioned, the natural next move is **not** another Olympic cycle (Brisbane 2032 is too far away to sustain narrative momentum). The right move is a **single-season spinoff or limited series** focused on one of the secondary storylines that emerged in seasons one and two — most obviously, the dynastic story of an American show jumping family (the Shulmans, or another barn discovered during production) across a single transformative year.

The Olympic format works once. The world of *The Circuit* can outlive it.
